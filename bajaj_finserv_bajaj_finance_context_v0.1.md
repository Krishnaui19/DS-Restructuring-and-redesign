> **Scope note:** The supplied URL, [bajajfinserv.in](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/), is the Bajaj Finserv digital platform. It presents products from Bajaj Finance as well as other Bajaj group companies, subsidiaries, partners, and marketplace providers. ## 1. Executive understanding

### What is Bajaj Finance?

Bajaj Finance Limited (BFL) is a deposit-taking non-banking financial company (NBFC-D), registered with the Reserve Bank of India. It is a subsidiary of Bajaj Finserv Limited and operates across retail, MSME, and commercial customer segments. Its core activities include lending and accepting public and corporate deposits. It also offers payment services and distributes or facilitates selected third-party financial products.

BFL has evolved from a two-wheeler finance business into a diversified financial-services company. Its portfolio spans consumer lending, personal loans, mortgages, SME/MSME lending, commercial lending, rural lending, vehicle finance, gold loans, loans against securities, deposits, and payment-related services.

### What is Bajaj Finserv?

Bajaj Finserv Limited is the wider financial-services group/holding company. The Bajaj Finserv website acts as a broad digital entry point to financial products and services across the group and its partners. The wider ecosystem includes lending, housing finance, insurance, investments, broking, health, payments, rewards, and commerce.

### The important distinction

| Name | What it refers to | How to think about it |
|---|---|---|
| Bajaj Group | The larger business group | Parent group identity |
| Bajaj Finserv Limited | Financial-services holding company | Group-level financial-services ecosystem |
| Bajaj Finance Limited (BFL) | Deposit-taking NBFC and lending business | Loans, deposits, payments, and related services |
| Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL) | Housing finance company | Home finance and mortgage-related products |
| Bajaj Financial Securities Limited (BFSL) | Securities/broking business | Demat, broking, and margin trading services |
| Other group companies and partners | Insurance, mutual funds, health, marketplace and other offerings | May be surfaced on Bajaj Finserv's platform but are not necessarily BFL products |

**UX implication:** A customer-facing category such as “Investments” or “Insurance” on the platform does not necessarily mean that Bajaj Finance itself manufactures or underwrites every product in that category. The interface should make the provider, issuer, lender, insurer, or partner clear at the point where that distinction matters.

## 2. Business model and customer promise

BFL's broad proposition is to serve financial needs across different stages of a customer's life and business. The company combines:

- **Credit:** access to funds for consumption, asset purchase, business operations, property, and liquidity.
- **Deposits:** fixed-deposit products for customers seeking returns on savings.
- **Payments:** wallet, UPI-related services, bill payments, and other payment facilities.
- **Point-of-sale finance:** EMI-based financing for products and services through merchant and e-commerce partners.
- **Investment-linked and partner services:** selected products and services surfaced through the wider platform.

The wider Bajaj Finserv ecosystem extends this proposition into protection, wealth creation, health, and securities services.

### Customer groups visible in the portfolio

1. **Individual / retail customers**
   - Salaried customers
   - Self-employed individuals
   - Customers seeking personal or lifestyle finance
   - Customers purchasing consumer durables, electronics, vehicles, or other products on EMI

2. **Professionals**
   - Doctors
   - Chartered Accountants
   - Lawyers
   - Other professional / self-employed segments where specific products are available

3. **Small businesses and MSMEs**
   - Traders
   - Shopkeepers and retailers
   - Manufacturers
   - Service providers
   - Small business owners

4. **Commercial and institutional customers**
   - Corporates
   - Vendors and suppliers
   - Businesses seeking working capital, term finance, or structured financing

5. **Rural customers**
   - Individuals and rural households
   - Rural micro, small, and medium businesses
   - Customers seeking consumer, vehicle, gold, and business finance

6. **Investors and depositors**
   - Retail fixed-deposit customers
   - Customers seeking loans against investments
   - Customers using broking, demat, or mutual-fund services through relevant group entities or partners

## 5. Important distinctions for information architecture

### 5.1 Product vs purpose vs audience vs collateral

These are different dimensions and should not be mixed in one category tree.

| Dimension | Example | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Financial product | Personal Loan | What is the financial product? |
| Customer purpose | Wedding expenses | Why does the customer need it? |
| Customer segment | Doctor / salaried / trader | Who is it designed for? |
| Security / collateral | Shares / property / gold | What backs the borrowing? |
| Financing structure | Secured / unsecured / Flexi | How is the credit structured? |
| Channel / context | At a merchant / online checkout | Where does the customer access it? |

For example, “Loan for Doctors” is an audience-specific proposition that may connect to more than one underlying product (business loan, personal loan, loan against property, or medical-practice finance). It should not automatically be treated as a wholly separate financial product family.

### 5.2 Secured vs unsecured

- **Unsecured lending:** The loan is not backed by a specific pledged asset. Examples include many personal loans and unsecured business loans.
- **Secured lending:** The borrower pledges or mortgages an asset as security. Examples include Loan Against Property, Loan Against Shares, Gold Loan, and Loan Against Car.

This is a useful filter or attribute across product families. It is not necessarily the best first-level customer-facing category for every customer.

### 5.3 Loan against an asset vs loan for purchasing an asset

These are opposite customer intents:

- **Loan for an asset:** Financing to purchase something, such as a Home Loan, Car Loan, or Two-wheeler Loan.
- **Loan against an asset:** Borrowing by using an asset already owned as collateral, such as Loan Against Property, Loan Against Shares, or Loan Against Car.

The wording “property finance” can cover both purchase finance and borrowing against owned property. Use more explicit labels where the distinction matters.

### 5.4 Balance transfer and top-up

Balance transfer is generally a facility / variant attached to an existing loan family, not a wholly separate asset or lending category. Top-up is an additional borrowing facility linked to an existing loan relationship. These should usually sit under the relevant product family.

### 5.5 Flexi Loan

Flexi is a loan structure / repayment and withdrawal proposition rather than a customer-purpose category. It can appear as a product variant under personal or business lending depending on the specific offer.

## 6. UX and website observations

Initial observations from the accessible navigation and product pages:

1. **The site is a large ecosystem, not a simple lender catalogue.** Lending, shopping, insurance, investments, payments, and partner services coexist in one platform.
2. **The navigation mixes different classification logics.** Some entries are products (Personal Loan), some are customer segments (Loans for Doctors), some are collateral types (Loan Against Shares), some are channels (Bajaj Mall), and some are purposes (Loan for Wedding).
3. **The same underlying product can appear in several contexts.** A doctor may encounter a personal loan, business loan, LAP, or medical-practice finance. A personal loan may also be surfaced under wedding, education, renovation, or medical needs.
4. **Product pages provide decision-support content.** Common elements include overview, features, eligibility, calculator, fees and charges, interest rates, application process, FAQs, and loan management.
5. **The platform has both acquisition and servicing journeys.** “Apply / Check Offer” is acquisition; “Manage your loan / FD / account” is servicing. These should be treated as distinct user tasks.
6. **Provider transparency is important.** Insurance and investment pages list multiple providers. The customer needs to know who is the lender, issuer, insurer, fund house, broker, or marketplace provider.
7. **Cross-sell is a visible ecosystem strategy.** Customers may move between loans, deposits, cards, payments, rewards, shopping, and third-party financial products.
8. **Digital and physical channels coexist.** The company's reporting describes app, website, social, branch, and distribution-point access.

These are initial design observations, not a completed usability evaluation.

## 7. Company context and strategy

Bajaj Finance's FY2025–26 annual report describes a portfolio of 27 products and 46 variants and positions the Bajaj Finance App as a central part of its platform-first strategy. It describes a “FINAI” transformation, with AI being used across customer acquisition, product discovery, onboarding, engagement, point-of-sale, service, and internal processes.

The annual report frames its long-range strategy around three priorities:

1. Customer centricity
2. Technology leadership in India
3. Becoming a low-risk company

This matters for UX because the platform is not simply a collection of product detail pages. It is intended to support discovery, conversion, servicing, engagement, and ongoing customer relationships across multiple financial needs.

## 10. Working principles for future design decisions

- Keep **Bajaj Finance** distinct from the broader **Bajaj Finserv** ecosystem.
- Separate product families from customer segments, purposes, collateral types, and channels.
- Use customer language for primary navigation; use financial/legal terminology where it is necessary for clarity and compliance.
- Do not create sibling categories that overlap conceptually (for example, “Property Loans” and “Asset-backed Loans”) without defining their relationship.
- Make it easy to compare products within a family.
- Explain the difference between borrowing to buy an asset and borrowing against an asset already owned.
- Treat calculators, eligibility, fees, application, and servicing as supporting tasks connected to a product, not as separate products.
- Clearly disclose the actual lender / issuer / insurer / provider wherever the platform distributes third-party or group-company offerings.
